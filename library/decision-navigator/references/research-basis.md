# Research Basis and Evidence Filter

## Purpose

This is not a literature review to repeat during every decision. Use it to understand why the process controls exist, to explain them when challenged, and to avoid encoding weak or overly precise claims from popular bias summaries.

## Core sources

### Systemizing decision criteria

Ray Dalio, *Principles* (official Principles.com pages). Practical relevance: write down decision criteria, convert recurring judgments into explicit principles/algorithms, consider expected value and unacceptable downside, and improve the rules through repeated cases.

- https://www.principles.com/principles/af0e5012-33f2-4e78-9755-5a12aee1c7fc
- https://www.principles.com/principles/faeb8045-7427-4b69-834c-9c3a8ca559c8/

Use the structural idea, not Dalio's specific organizational doctrine as universal truth.

### Inside view vs outside view

Daniel Kahneman & Dan Lovallo (1993), "Timid Choices and Bold Forecasts: A Cognitive Perspective on Risk Taking," *Management Science*, 39(1), 17-31. Decision makers often treat cases as unique, neglect past statistics, and build optimistic forecasts from internal plans.

- DOI: 10.1287/mnsc.39.1.17
- https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.39.1.17

Control: reference classes/base rates before detailed internal narratives when forecasting matters.

### Process review beats bias awareness alone

Daniel Kahneman, Dan Lovallo & Olivier Sibony (2011), "Before You Make That Big Decision," *Harvard Business Review*. Their central practical argument is that knowing bias names is insufficient; important decisions need process checks that expose predictable distortions.

- https://hbr.org/2011/06/the-big-idea-before-you-make-that-big-decision

Control: redesign the decision process rather than relying on self-awareness.

### Reference class forecasting

Bent Flyvbjerg (2006), "From Nobel Prize to Project Management: Getting Risks Right," and later work on project biases. Reference class forecasting uses the outcomes of comparable projects to counter optimism and strategic misrepresentation.

- DOI: 10.1177/875697280603700302
- https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A4bb1f512-941b-4595-b1be-939f3679b677

A 2025 critical review notes that reference class forecasting has important applicability and validity questions; it should not be treated as mechanically correct when the reference class is poor.

- https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09537287.2025.2578708

Control: use several credible analogues, explain comparability limits, and do not force a base rate where none exists.

### Premortem / prospective hindsight

Gary Klein (2007), "Performing a Project Premortem," *Harvard Business Review*. A premortem asks a team to assume the planned project failed and generate plausible reasons, making it easier to surface concerns before commitment.

- https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem

Related foundation: Mitchell, Russo & Pennington (1989), "Back to the Future: Temporal Perspective in the Explanation of Events," *Journal of Behavioral Decision Making*.

- DOI: 10.1002/bdm.3960020103

Control: for material decisions, assume failure and generate the most plausible causes.

### Escalation of commitment / sunk cost

Barry Staw (1976; 1981) showed how responsibility for an initial choice and negative outcomes can increase subsequent commitment to a failing course of action. Later reviews distinguish sunk-cost effects from the broader escalation process.

- 1976 DOI: 10.1016/0030-5073(76)90005-2
- 1981 DOI: 10.5465/AMR.1981.4285694

Control: precommit limits and evaluate continuation using future incremental cost/benefit and opportunity cost.

### Anchoring and groups

de Wilde, Ten Velden & De Dreu (2018), "The anchoring-bias in groups," *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology*, 76, 116-126. Groups can remain susceptible to anchors; process accountability reduced anchoring in their studies.

- DOI: 10.1016/j.jesp.2018.02.001

Control: form initial judgments/ranges independently where possible and make the process accountable.

## Evidence corrections to popular claims

### Loss aversion is not a universal fixed multiplier

Two 2024 meta-analyses reach different aggregate estimates depending on methods and included studies. Brown et al. analyze 607 estimates from 150 articles and report a mean coefficient near 1.96; Walasek, Mullett & Stewart, using a narrower risky-choice model-fitting set, report a much smaller median near 1.31 and emphasize heterogeneity and data limitations.

- Brown et al., *Journal of Economic Literature* 62(2): https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20221698
- Walasek et al., *Journal of Economic Psychology* 103: DOI 10.1016/j.joep.2024.102740

Implication: preserve the qualitative possibility that losses are overweighted; do not encode "losses hurt 3X" as a universal fact.

### Social loafing is real but highly moderated

Karau & Williams (1993) meta-analyzed 78 studies and found a robust overall social-loafing effect, while identifying strong moderators such as evaluation potential, expected coworker performance, task meaningfulness, and culture. Later integrative reviews also emphasize that motivation gains can occur.

- DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.65.4.681
- 2020 integrative review: DOI 10.1016/B978-0-12-849867-5.00001-X

Implication: make ownership and contribution visible; do not encode exact effort percentages by team size.

### Decision fatigue should not be reduced to blood sugar

Modern systematic reviews find evidence consistent with decision fatigue in some domains but describe measurement and causal uncertainty. The simplistic claim that low glucose explains the effect and eating restores willpower should not be used as an operating rule.

- 2025 healthcare systematic review: PubMed PMID 40591577
- 2025 clinical review: PubMed PMID 39922690

Implication: reduce unnecessary repeated decisions, use defaults/checklists for routine choices, and schedule important judgment sensibly without pretending a snack is a validated debiasing tool.

### "Because" is context-sensitive, not a magic compliance word

The famous Langer copy-machine finding depended on request size and context; even summaries of the original experiment show the content-free reason did not help for the larger favor.

Implication: treat justification as evidence only when the reason itself is relevant and substantive.

## India operating context

SIDBI's FY2024-25 annual report estimates an addressable MSME credit gap of roughly ₹30 lakh crore and identifies timely/adequate credit access as a continuing challenge. Its MSME Outlook surveys also track debtor-realization delays and credit access.

- https://www.sidbi.in/annualreport/AnnualReport202425/msme-outlook.php
- https://www.sidbi.in/en/msme-outlook-survey

The Ministry of MSME notified revised guidelines on 30 June 2026 requiring all operating CPSEs to onboard RBI-authorised TReDS platforms and settle MSME invoices through them, announced publicly on 10 July 2026. The stated reason is that delayed payment persistently locks up MSME working capital.

- PIB release, 10 July 2026: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2283195
- Business Standard, 10 July 2026: https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/govt-mandates-cpses-to-route-msme-invoice-settlements-through-treds-126071000875_1.html

Implication for Indian service/MSME decisions: receivable behavior, statutory burden, local execution capacity, and working-capital exposure deserve explicit treatment rather than being buried inside a generic strategy checklist.

## General evidence rule

When the skill encounters a precise psychological claim:

1. Ask whether the precision matters to the decision.
2. If not, use the robust directional insight only.
3. If it matters, verify with current primary or high-quality review evidence.
4. Distinguish replicated effect, context-dependent effect, heuristic, and anecdote.
5. Never let a memorable bias label substitute for evidence about the actual decision.

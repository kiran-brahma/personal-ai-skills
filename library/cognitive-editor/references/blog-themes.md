# Personal Blog Core Themes — Routing Reference

Every piece must belong to ONE and only ONE theme. If it fits multiple, it is miswritten or misfiled.

---

## 1. The Operator Stack (Separate Project — Not the Blog)

**Purpose:** Execution, systems, and scars.

**What lives here:**
- Decision frameworks forged under pressure
- Build logs (ERP, ops systems, AI workflows)
- System design for ops-heavy, people-dependent businesses

**Tone:** Precise. Scar-driven. Non-negotiable judgment.

**Rule:** If it teaches *how to execute* or documents systems-in-progress, it belongs here — not on the personal blog.

**Litmus test:** Does this remove a future decision or translate judgment into a system?

---

## 2. Personal Writings (kiranbrahma.com)

**Purpose:** Reflective synthesis of thinking shaped by experience.

**What lives here:**
- Essays on entrepreneurship, sales, marketing, AI, and decision-making
- Writing that shows *how thinking evolved*, not how systems were built
- Ideas being shaped before they harden into doctrine

**What does NOT belong:**
- Raw emotions written in the moment
- Step-by-step execution guides
- Reusable frameworks

**Tone:** Calm, reflective, judgment-aware.

**Litmus test:** If the piece explains *why you now think differently*, it belongs here.

---

## 3. Learning from Books

**Purpose:** Extraction of thinking from books that survived real-world application.

**What lives here:**
- Deep dives into books that altered judgment or decisions
- Distilled frameworks tested in practice

**What does NOT belong:**
- Chapter-by-chapter summaries
- Generic book reviews
- Books that only sounded good but changed nothing

**Litmus test:** If the book changed how you *decide or act*, it qualifies.

---

## 4. Study Guides

**Purpose:** Reusable decision scaffolding under constraint.

**What lives here:**
- Compact reference documents
- Aggregated patterns across hiring, product, pricing, marketing, ops
- Notes you personally return to when judgment matters

**Litmus test:** If it's meant to be *revisited during a decision*, it belongs here.

---

## 5. Understanding AI

**Purpose:** Conceptual grounding for the use of AI.

**What lives here:**
- Technical foundations of AI
- Economic realities and trade-offs
- Where AI works, fails, and creates risk

**Boundary:** Application, workflows, and execution stay in *The Operator Stack*.

**Tone:** Clear, skeptical, grounded.

**Litmus test:** If it helps you *understand AI* rather than *use AI*, it belongs here.

**Hard rule:** NO personal operational content. The routing distinction between "Understanding AI" and "Operator Stack" is a structural boundary, not a tone choice.

---

## 6. Journal

**Purpose:** Raw signal capture.

**What lives here:**
- Time-bound thoughts written close to the moment
- Confusion, early insights, unresolved questions
- Observations before clarity exists

**Litmus test:** If it feels incomplete but important *right now*, it belongs here.

---

## 7. Things Worth Reading

**Purpose:** Curation with judgment.

**Fixed entry format:**
- Title — Author
- Why it mattered (2–3 sentences)
- What to be careful about (1 sentence)

**Hard rules:** Not a reading list. Not summaries. Low volume, high bar.

**Litmus test:** If it didn't change a decision, it doesn't stay.

---

## 8. Mental Models — Thinking References

**Purpose:** Personal latticework of thinking tools — collected, tested, and linked over time.

**What lives here:**
- Atomic notes on individual mental models — one model per piece
- Each grounded in a real example
- Links to other mental models in the library

**Usage Template:**
- Model Name
- Context Behind the Model
- How to Apply (the mechanism)
- Where to Apply (the domain)
- Where it Fails (the honest part)
- Connects to (links to other atomic notes)

**Litmus test:** If you can state what the model gets wrong — and show one real example of it working — it belongs here.

---

## Past Failures (The Operator Stack sub-theme)

Case studies of documented business failures (e.g., Pets.com). Routes to The Operator Stack under "Past Failures" theme. Series routing question is closed.

---

## Global Enforcement Rules

- Every piece belongs to ONE and only ONE theme.
- If a piece fits multiple themes, it must be rewritten or split.
- Older articles should be reclassified, not force-fitted.
- Blending series identities is a routing problem, not a writing problem.

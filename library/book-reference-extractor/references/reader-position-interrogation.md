# Reader Position Interrogation

Use this protocol after building the private author map and before drafting the long-form document.

## Purpose

Establish the reader's actual position, not a plausible reaction supplied by the AI. The interview should uncover acceptance, resistance, uncertainty, experience, failure conditions, practical relevance, costs, and revision criteria. It is complete only when the reader confirms a shared understanding.

## Grilling-with-docs method

Apply the `grill-with-docs` workflow in-line for this stage. Use its `grilling` discipline to map the reader's position as a decision tree:

1. Put the claims and tensions that need judgment at the root.
2. Treat each answer as a branch that exposes the next decisions beneath it.
3. Ask the whole current frontier in one numbered round.
4. Keep questions whose prerequisites are unresolved for a later round.
5. Recompute the frontier after every answer.
6. Stop when every material branch is resolved or explicitly deferred, then present the shared-understanding checkpoint.

Use `domain-modeling` to sharpen overloaded terms and name distinctions that must survive into the final prose. Record those terms in the private map as they become settled.

The general `grilling` workflow can recommend answers for design decisions. This interview is a reader assessment, so omit recommended answers, persuasion, and praise. A recommendation would steer the reader's position.

## Questioning sequence

1. Select two to four supplied claims or tensions that most require the reader's judgment.
2. Ask four or five precise questions in the first round.
3. Ask later-round questions only when they resolve a material ambiguity, contradiction, unstated condition, unsupported confidence, or missing countercase.
4. Use no fixed maximum number of rounds. Stop when the tree is settled or the reader chooses to defer.

## Conduct

- Anchor each question to a specific author claim, observation, mechanism, phrase, or tension.
- Ask for the exact component at issue: observation, mechanism, scope, conclusion, implication, cost, or evidence.
- Ask one clear question at a time within the numbered set.
- Ask rather than teach. If the reader requests clarification, use the supplied source and preserve the author's wording before asking again.
- Treat “I do not know,” “not enough experience,” silence, and deferral as valid unresolved states.
- Treat existing reader notes as hypotheses to verify, not as a finished assessment.
- Keep the author's case and the reader's position separate while questioning.

## What to uncover

Use only dimensions relevant to the supplied material:

- Which part the reader accepts: observation, mechanism, scope, conclusion, or implication.
- Which part the reader rejects or doubts.
- Experience that makes the idea credible or suspicious.
- Conditions under which the idea may fail.
- The problem the idea helps the reader name or examine.
- The benefit the reader expects if the idea holds.
- The cost, danger, or neglected variable created by applying it.
- Conflict with another belief or operating principle.
- Evidence or experience that would change the reader's view.
- Questions the reader cannot yet answer.

## Preferred question forms

- In P2, which part do you accept: the observation, the proposed mechanism, or the conclusion?
- Where would this proposition fail in your operating context?
- What experience makes this argument credible to you?
- What experience makes you suspicious of it?
- What problem becomes easier to describe after reading this idea?
- What benefit would you expect if you acted as though it were true?
- What cost could arise if you applied it beyond its valid range?
- What does this proposition cause you to underweight or ignore?
- You appear to accept both claims, but they imply different actions under pressure. Which takes priority, and under what condition?
- What evidence would make you revise your current position?

## Avoid

- Do you agree with the author?
- How can you apply this?
- What did you find useful?
- Would you like some recommendations?
- Here is what I think you should consider.
- Questions that bundle an argument, a suggested answer, and a request for confirmation.

Replace broad prompts with questions that identify the exact disputed component, condition, cost, or evidence.

## Shared-understanding checkpoint

After the frontier is empty, show only confirmed material:

- the author's case as grounded in the supplied material;
- the reader's stance and meaningful wording;
- the conditions and experiences attached to that stance;
- settled terms and distinctions;
- disagreement, uncertainty, and deferred questions.

Ask the reader to confirm or correct the checkpoint. Draft only after confirmation. If the reader corrects it, update the tree and repeat the checkpoint. If the reader stops, preserve the checkpoint as an interim state.

## Recording answers

Preserve meaningful wording under `Reader's own words`. Use `AI summary` only when a concise paraphrase improves retrieval, and label it. Do not write in the first person on the reader's behalf unless directly quoting or closely preserving supplied wording.

Mark deferred items as `unanswered` and retain the original question under `Open questions`. Do not convert non-response into agreement or disagreement.

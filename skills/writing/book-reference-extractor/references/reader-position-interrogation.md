# Reader Position Interrogation

Use this protocol after completing the author record and before recording the reader's independent assessment.

## Purpose

Elicit the reader's actual position. Do not seek agreement with the author or the AI. Establish a clear map of acceptance, resistance, uncertainty, experience, failure conditions, practical relevance, and revision criteria.

## Questioning sequence

1. Select the two to four propositions or tensions that most require the reader's judgment.
2. Ask four or five initial questions in one round.
3. After the reader answers, ask no more than three follow-ups.
4. Follow up only to clarify a material ambiguity, contradiction, unstated condition, unsupported confidence, or missing countercase.
5. Stop questioning when the reader's position is clear enough to record, or when the reader chooses to defer.

## Conduct

- Ask one clear question at a time within the numbered set.
- Anchor each question to a specific proposition, observation, or tension.
- Ask rather than teach.
- Do not recommend an action.
- Do not defend or attack the author.
- Do not supply candidate answers unless the reader explicitly asks for clarification.
- Do not praise, reassure, or frame an answer as correct.
- Do not complete the reader's reasoning.
- Do not turn uncertainty into a conclusion.
- Accept "I do not know," "not enough experience," and silence as valid unresolved states.

## What to uncover

Use only the dimensions relevant to the material:

- Which part the reader accepts: observation, mechanism, scope, conclusion, or implication.
- Which part the reader rejects or doubts.
- Experience that makes the idea credible or suspicious.
- Conditions under which the idea may fail.
- The problem the idea helps the reader name or examine.
- The benefit the reader expects if the idea holds.
- The cost, danger, or neglected variable created by applying it.
- Conflict with another belief or operating principle.
- Evidence or experience that would change the reader's view.
- Questions the reader cannot yet answer.

## Preferred question forms

- In P2, which part do you accept: the observation, the proposed mechanism, or the conclusion?
- Where would this proposition fail in your operating context?
- What experience makes this argument credible to you?
- What experience makes you suspicious of it?
- What problem becomes easier to describe after reading this idea?
- What benefit would you expect if you acted as though it were true?
- What cost could arise if you applied it beyond its valid range?
- What does this proposition cause you to underweight or ignore?
- You appear to accept both P1 and P4, but they imply different actions under pressure. Which takes priority, and under what condition?
- What evidence would make you revise your current position?

## Avoid

- Do you agree with the author?
- How can you apply this?
- What did you find useful?
- Would you like some recommendations?
- Here is what I think you should consider.

Replace broad prompts with questions that identify the exact disputed component, condition, cost, or evidence.

## Recording answers

Preserve meaningful wording under `Reader's own words`.

Use `AI summary` only for a faithful, concise paraphrase. Do not write in the first person on the reader's behalf unless directly quoting or closely preserving supplied wording.

Mark deferred items as `unanswered` and retain the original question under `Open questions`.

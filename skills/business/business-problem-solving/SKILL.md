# Business problem solving

Turn a broad business problem into a small set of answerable questions and a usable work plan. The workflow supports the user’s thinking; it does not replace their judgement.

## Phase 1: Problem definition

Ask for the problem one piece at a time: basic situation, affected person or unit, desired outcome, scope, constraints, deadline, and stakeholders. Rewrite the problem without prescribing a solution. Make success measurable where possible.

If the user is unsure, offer two or three clearly labelled interpretations or a concrete example. Mark the suggestion as a hypothesis and ask which, if any, fits. Do not fill missing facts with confidence.

## Phase 2: Diagnosis and structure

Use the least elaborate diagnosis that changes the work:

- **Clear:** cause and response are established.
- **Complicated:** expert analysis can reveal a likely answer.
- **Complex:** the answer must be learned through bounded experiments.
- **Chaotic:** immediate action must first stabilise the situation.

For exploration, build an issue tree. For a claim to test, build a hypothesis tree. Check that branches do not overlap and that the important problem space is covered. MECE is a review question, not a reason to force artificial categories.

## Phase 3: Prioritisation

List the questions, not just the symptoms. Rank them by decision impact, evidence availability, time or cost to answer, reversibility, and downside if wrong. Use an impact/ease matrix only when it clarifies the order. Include confidence and the evidence behind each rating.

Prioritise questions that can change the decision. Do not spend days refining a tree when one customer call, payment record, site observation, or unit-margin calculation can settle the branch.

## Phase 4: Work plan

For each priority question, specify the question, hypothesis if applicable, evidence required, source or method, analysis or experiment, output, decision it informs, and stop condition.

Use primary documents and direct business records where available. Label estimates. Do not recommend a data source without saying what it can and cannot establish.

## Phase 5: Synthesis

Produce a report with this order:

1. Situation in one sentence.
2. Confirmed facts.
3. Structure and key questions.
4. Inferences and assumptions.
5. Evidence gaps and falsifiers.
6. Priorities and work plan.
7. Downside scenario.
8. Decision or next action.

Name the phase and pause at each meaningful gate. The user may revise, revisit, pause, or proceed. Do not force completion merely because the template has been filled.
